THE BIG PICTURE (use this for the Abstract and Introduction)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hate speech against LGBTQ+ people on Indian social media is often written
in Hindi or in Hinglish, a casual mix of Hindi and English typed in Latin
letters. Deleting posts doesn't change minds. Counter-narratives can:
calm, respectful replies that challenge the hateful claim and support the
targeted group. Writing them by hand doesn't scale, and asking a large
language model (LLM) to write them causes three problems:

   1. The model makes up facts and statistics.
   2. Replies are shallow and one-sided, for example only "that's wrong"
      or only "be kind", with no reasoning behind them.
   3. Code-mixed Hindi-Hinglish is a low-resource setting, so there is
      little data or tooling to work with.

Our idea: a good human counter-speaker does three things before replying.
They know the facts, they look at the post from several angles (rights,
harm, culture, empathy), and they plan what to say. We build a system that
does the same:

   - a new 1,550-pair Hindi-Hinglish hate-speech / counter-narrative dataset;
   - a knowledge graph of facts from trustworthy sources (UN, courts, NGOs,
     official and peer-reviewed documents);
   - several reasoning "perspective agents" that each analyse the post and
     agree on a plan before anything is written;
   - a final counter-narrative that cites its evidence.

We compare it with a plain LLM and with a knowledge-graph-only version,
using automatic metrics and human judges.

Draft abstract (edit freely; keep the results sentence blank until the
results are in):

   "Counter-narratives offer a constructive alternative to removing hate
   speech, but generating them automatically for low-resource, code-mixed
   languages remains difficult: large language models hallucinate facts
   and produce generic, single-viewpoint replies. We study homophobic hate
   speech in Hindi and Hinglish and make three contributions. First, we
   build a 1,550-pair hate-speech / counter-narrative dataset through five
   rounds of LLM-assisted expansion with human review, starting from posts
   collected on social media. Second, we construct a knowledge graph of
   claims, entities and evidence from 90 authoritative documents. Third,
   we propose MP-KG-RAG, which retrieves evidence from this graph, analyses
   each post through multiple value-oriented reasoning perspectives,
   synthesises a response plan, and only then generates a cited
   counter-narrative. Compared with zero-shot and chain-of-thought LLM
   baselines and a knowledge-graph-only system, MP-KG-RAG [RESULTS: e.g.
   improves factuality and persuasiveness while maintaining safety]
   according to automatic metrics and human evaluation."

STEP 2: KNOWLEDGE GRAPH CONSTRUCTION
==========================================================================

WHAT WE DID
An LLM can only argue well if it has facts it can rely on. So we built a
domain knowledge graph (KG) from trustworthy documents: 90 sources covering
LGBTQ+ rights, health, law, discrimination and hate speech. They include
UN agency reports, court judgments, official / government documents,
NGO reports, surveys and peer-reviewed papers. Every source has an
authority score (0.72 to 1.00) that reflects how trustworthy it is.

An LLM (Qwen3.5-4B) read the documents and extracted:
   - claims: factual statements, each tied to the exact passage it came
     from, so every fact can be traced to its source;
   - entities: groups, organisations, laws, places, concepts (e.g.
     "transgender people", "Section 377", "WHO", "same-sex marriage").

These became nodes in a graph, connected by typed links.

HOW IT WORKS
   1. Collect and clean documents. Only valid, readable PDFs are kept;
      broken or duplicate files are removed. Each document is
      fingerprinted (SHA-256) so the corpus is reproducible.
   2. Split into passages. 6,431 overlapping passages of 500-900 tokens,
      each linked to its document and page.
   3. Extract claims with the LLM. For each passage the model returns up
      to 4 claims in a fixed JSON format: the claim, the entities it
      mentions, and its start and end position in the text. Badly formed
      output gets one repair attempt. 21,592 claims were extracted.
   4. Find entities. 34,813 entity mentions, merged into about 1,500
      distinct entities.
   5. Build the graph.
        Document -> Page -> Passage -> Claim -> Entity
      Links: a document has pages; a page has passages; a passage asserts
      claims; a claim mentions entities; claims relate to other claims.
      Final size: about 35,600 nodes and 67,700 links.
   6. Link each hate-speech post into the graph. For every post, the LLM
      identifies which graph entities the post is about (e.g. the target
      "Transgender Individuals") and describes the kind of harmful claim
      being made. The model must choose from the graph's existing entity
      list; it cannot invent new ones. This "query signature" is how a
      post finds its relevant facts.

How this maps onto your supervisor's Step 2 list:
   Hate targets             -> entity nodes for each target group; each
                               post is linked to its target entities
   Harmful claims/narratives -> the claim the post makes (query
                               signature: claim type, polarity, stance)
   Supporting/refuting facts -> claim nodes from the 90 sources, each
                               tied to its exact passage
   Social/cultural context  -> NGO, survey and UN documents on social
                               context; the cultural-context perspective
                               (Step 3)
   Counter-speech resources -> high-authority passages (rights law, health
                               bodies, court rulings) used as citations

   - Source selection and authority scores: a table of source types and

   - LLM-based extraction: model, output format, the rule that every claim

STEP 3: MULTI-PERSPECTIVE REASONING
==========================================================================

WHAT WE DID
Instead of asking the model to reply straight away, we ask it to think
about the post from five viewpoints first. Each viewpoint is a separate
"agent" (the same LLM given a different role and goal). Each agent reads
the post, its target and the retrieved evidence, and writes a short
structured analysis. A planner agent then combines the five analyses into
one response plan. Only after that is the counter-narrative written
(Step 4). This is the "reason first, then respond" idea behind
multi-perspective value alignment and chain-of-thought reasoning.

HOW IT WORKS
The five perspectives as implemented, and how they match your supervisor's
list:

   Implemented         Its goal                            Supervisor's name
   fact_checking       correct only claims the evidence    Fact-checking
                       directly supports
   legal_rights        use only rights claims backed by    Human rights
                       sources
   harm_reduction      avoid amplifying harmful language;  Social harmony
                       de-escalate
   persuasion          suggest an empathetic, convincing   Empathy / support
                       response
   cultural_context    give respectful social and          Educational
                       cultural context

Each agent returns the same structured analysis:
   - which harmful claims in the post to address;
   - its reasoning (rationale);
   - which evidence passages support its view (by ID: E1, E2, ...);
   - guidance for the reply;
   - risk flags (e.g. "avoid repeating the slur");
   - a confidence score from 0 to 1.

The planner reads all five analyses and returns the plan:
   - the main claim to focus on;
   - which evidence to use;
   - the steps of the reply;
   - the tone;
   - factual constraints (what may and may not be claimed);
   - safety constraints.

Chain of thought: every agent first thinks privately (a short reasoning
step of up to 192 tokens), then writes its structured answer. The
reasoning is saved separately, so it can be inspected later and quoted as
examples in the paper.

Value alignment: each perspective stands for a value the reply should
respect (truthfulness, rights, harmony, empathy, understanding). The
planner has to reconcile them, for example being factual without being
harsh, or empathetic without inventing facts.

WRITE IN PAPER (Method, part 1)
   - Motivation: single-pass generation produces one-dimensional replies;
     human counter-speakers weigh several values.
   - The perspectives: a table of name, value, goal and what it outputs.
   - The planner and the plan structure.
   - Chain of thought: thinking before structured output.
   - A worked example figure: one hateful post -> five short perspective
     notes -> plan -> final counter-narrative. This is often the most
     persuasive figure in such papers. Take it from a real run.

STEP 4: KNOWLEDGE-GROUNDED COUNTER-NARRATIVE GENERATION
==========================================================================

WHAT WE DID
For each hateful post the system:
   (a) retrieves the most relevant facts from the knowledge base and graph;
   (b) runs the multi-perspective reasoning and planning from Step 3 on
       them;
   (c) writes the final counter-narrative, citing the evidence it used,
       e.g. "...as recognised by the WHO [E2]".

HOW IT WORKS
(a) Retrieving evidence: three searches combined.
   - Meaning-based search (BGE-M3 embeddings): finds passages with
     similar meaning even when the wording differs.
   - Keyword search (BM25): finds passages sharing important words with
     the post and target.
   - Graph search: starts from the passages found above and follows graph
     links (up to 2 steps) to related claims and entities. Graph results
     are scored higher when they match the post's target entities and the
     type of claim being made, and when they come from more authoritative
     sources.
   - The three result lists are merged (reciprocal rank fusion), and a
     second model (BGE reranker) checks how relevant each passage really
     is to the post. Only passages with relevance >= 0.55 are kept, at
     most 5.
   - If nothing passes, the system refuses to answer rather than produce
     an ungrounded reply. This is a safety feature worth highlighting.

(b) Evidence ledger: the chosen passages are numbered E1 to E5. The model
    may only cite these IDs, which makes made-up sources impossible to
    hide: every citation can be checked.

(c) Writing the reply: the final step receives the plan and the evidence
    and must produce:
   - the counter-narrative;
   - the evidence IDs it used;
   - the factual claims it makes;
   - safety notes.
   Any factual sentence must carry a citation. If the evidence doesn't
   support a confident reply, the model must use a fixed polite refusal
   ("I cannot verify this from the available evidence.").

How each target quality of the counter-narrative is achieved:
   Factually grounded  evidence-only facts, citations E1-E5, refusal when
                       evidence is missing, citation checking (Step 5)
   Empathetic          empathy/support perspective; tone set in the plan
   Non-confrontational social harmony perspective; planner's safety
                       constraints ("avoid amplification", no slurs)
   Context-aware       target and category of the post; cultural /
                       educational perspective; plan built for this post

Example from a test run (English input):
   Post: "I don't think same-sex couples should be allowed to adopt
         children."
   Reply (summary): same-sex couples can legally adopt in 28 UN member
   states; international human-rights principles (Yogyakarta Principle
   24) support the right to form a family; denying adoption leaves
   children without legal protection. All five evidence passages cited.
   [Use examples from the main experiment in the paper, ideally Hindi and
   Hinglish ones.]

STEP 5: EVALUATION
==========================================================================

WHAT WE COMPARE
   1. Standard LLM: the same Qwen model replying directly.
        a. Zero-shot: just the post and an instruction.
        b. Simple chain of thought: the model thinks briefly, then replies.
      Prompts were written by hand, not generated with AI, so the baseline
      prompts don't leak extra knowledge.
   2. KG-based (KG-RAG): retrieved evidence plus one generation step with a
      fixed simple plan. No perspectives.
   3. Multi-perspective value-aligned KG (MP-KG-RAG, ours): retrieved
      evidence, five perspectives, planner, final reply.

   Comparing 1 vs 2 shows what knowledge adds. Comparing 2 vs 3 shows what
   multi-perspective reasoning adds on top of knowledge. That is the core
   argument of the paper.

   An extra experiment: run retrieval with and without the graph search,
   to show the graph itself helps (more relevant, more authoritative
   evidence).

   Implementation note: the code also has a few-shot baseline (two fixed
   examples), and the zero-shot baseline already uses the model's thinking
   mode, which effectively makes it a chain-of-thought baseline. Decide
   with your supervisor whether the paper reports zero-shot without
   thinking plus zero-shot with thinking (CoT), or zero-shot plus few-shot.

HOW TO EVALUATE: the supervisor's criteria and how to measure each
   Persuasiveness  Human rating (1-5). No reliable automatic metric
                   exists; this is mainly a human criterion.
   Helpfulness     Human rating (1-5).
   Diversity       Automatic: distinct-1 / distinct-2 (share of unique
                   words or word pairs across replies) and self-BLEU
                   (lower = more varied). Not yet in the code; quick to add.
                   The repetition-rate and novelty metrics already used for
                   the dataset versions can be reused here.
   Factuality      Automatic: citation precision and recall. A
                   multilingual NLI model (XLM-RoBERTa-large-XNLI) checks
                   whether each cited passage actually supports the
                   sentence that cites it. NLI thresholds are calibrated
                   for English, Hindi and Tamil. Plus a human factuality
                   rating.
   Safety          Automatic: Detoxify toxicity score (lower is better;
                   English only, so note this limitation for Hindi). Plus a
                   human safety rating.
   Similarity to   chrF (character-level, works for Devanagari and
   reference       Hinglish), BERTScore (multilingual), ROUGE-L, METEOR.
                   These only show closeness to one reference reply, so
                   treat them as secondary.
   Human           Blinded study: up to 200 samples, stratified by target
   evaluation      and script, all methods mixed and anonymised, at least 2
                   raters. Criteria built into the code: politeness and
                   respect, helpfulness, factuality, safety, empathy,
                   fluency, inclusivity, non-confrontational, contextual
                   coherence, persuasiveness. Report agreement between
                   raters (weighted Cohen's kappa and Krippendorff's alpha).

   Statistics: compare methods on the same posts with a paired
   permutation test (10,000 permutations), correct for multiple
   comparisons (Benjamini-Hochberg), and report 95% bootstrap confidence
   intervals.

LIMITATIONS TO DISCUSS (shows maturity; reviewers expect it)
==========================================================================
   - Language gap: the knowledge sources are mostly English, while most
     posts are Hinglish. Retrieval sometimes finds no evidence for Hinglish
     posts, and the system then refuses. Future work: translate or
     transliterate posts before searching, or add Hindi sources.
   - Dataset: GPT-assisted generation may carry GPT's style and biases;
     the non-homophobic class is small; human review was done by
     [number] reviewer(s).
   - Factuality checks confirm a reply matches its cited source, not that
     the source is true in the real world.
   - Toxicity scoring is English-only.
   - The knowledge graph is built automatically, so some extracted claims
     contain errors (about 5% mismatched their source passage).
   - No model was trained or fine-tuned: all gains come from knowledge
     grounding and structured reasoning. This is also a strength (cheap,
     transparent, easy to update).
   - Ethics: hateful examples are used only for research; the system is
     meant to assist human moderators, not to post replies automatically.
