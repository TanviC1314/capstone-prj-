================================================================================
1. PROBLEM AND CONTRIBUTIONS
================================================================================
Task: given a homophobic hate-speech (from dataset made using(English, Hindi in Devanagari, or
code-mixed Hinglish in Latin script) and its target group, generate a
counter-narrative (CN): a respectful, non-confrontational, factually grounded
reply that challenges the hateful claim without repeating slurs.

Problems with plain LLM generation that the method addresses:
  - Hallucinated facts and unverifiable statistics in CNs.
  - Generic, one-dimensional replies (only "facts" or only "empathy").
  - Little support for low-resource, code-mixed Hindi-Hinglish input.
  - No traceability from a CN sentence back to a source.

Candidate contributions (to state in the paper):
  C1. A 1,550-pair Hindi-Hinglish homophobic hate-speech / counter-narrative
      dataset, built in five human-reviewed iterations (Section 2).
  C2. A semantic knowledge graph of 90 authoritative documents (NGO, UN,
      court, official, peer-reviewed) with span-grounded claims (Section 3).
  C3. A hybrid retriever: dense + BM25 + typed graph expansion, fused by
      weighted reciprocal rank fusion and filtered by a cross-encoder with an
      abstention threshold (Section 4).
  C4. MP-KG-RAG: five perspective agents, a planner and a final synthesiser,
      all with schema-constrained JSON output and closed-ledger citations
      (Section 5).
  C5. A citation-verification and evaluation protocol: closed evidence
      ledger, NLI-based joint and leave-one-out entailment with per-language
      calibrated thresholds, paired permutation tests, and blinded human
      rating with agreement statistics (Sections 6-7).

2. DATASET
================================================================================
2.1 Schema
  ID | Text (hate speech) | Category | Target | Counter Narrative (reference)

2.2 Construction (iterative, human-in-the-loop)
  Seed (V1, 50 rows) -> V2 (100) -> V3 (200) -> V4 (400) -> V5 (800)
  -> Final_Dataset (1,550). Each expansion was LLM-assisted and then
  human-reviewed: reviewers kept, edited or discarded each pair
  (text-modified and CN-modified flags, status, notes). Edit effort was
  measured with HTER.

  A separate design (Kaggle notebook) describes synthetic generation with
  lukey03/Qwen3.5-9B-abliterated in 4-bit NF4 (fallback
  wangzhang/Qwen3.5-4B-abliterated), with quotas by language (English /
  Hindi / Hinglish), platform style (X/Twitter, Instagram comment, meme
  caption, YouTube, forum), abuse type and severity; JSON-array output;
  validation (required fields, language heuristics, no URLs, handles or
  real people); exact and near-duplicate removal (character n-gram TF-IDF
  cosine); resumable checkpoints. Check which of these steps produced the
  final rows before describing them in the paper.

3. KNOWLEDGE BASE AND SEMANTIC KNOWLEDGE GRAPH
================================================================================
3.1 Corpus
  90 documents (an earlier 95-file corpus, extended by a "KB25" pack of 25
  catalogued PDFs; content de-duplicated by SHA-256). Every document records
  URL, publisher, title, year, byte size and SHA-256. PDFs are accepted only
  with a valid PDF signature, a non-trivial size and a successful parse;
  failed, HTML, interstitial, corrupt or encrypted files are quarantined.
  Parser: PyMuPDF 1.26.5, page-aware.

  Each document gets a source type and an authority score in [0, 1]. By chunk:
  NGO report 2,445 | official 1,226 | court 991 | peer-reviewed 751 |
  UN agency 688 | survey 304 | education 25 | government 1.
  Authority scores observed: 0.72-1.00 (0.75: 2,749 chunks; 0.95: 1,742;
  1.00: 1,226; 0.92: 689). Retrieval only uses chunks with authority >= 0.60.

3.2 Chunking
  Token-based chunks of 500-900 tokens with 100-token overlap
  (config: semantic.min_tokens / max_tokens / overlap_tokens).
  Result: 6,431 chunks; median 2,812 characters (90th percentile 5,351,
  max 8,864). Each chunk keeps its document ID, page number, character and
  token spans, and SHA-256 hashes of the document, page and chunk.

3.3 Claim extraction (LLM, schema-constrained)
  Model: Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B (revision 3764fa35...), bitsandbytes NF4 4-bit,
  served by vLLM (8 concurrent requests), prompt "semantic-claims.v1".
  Output schema per chunk:
    {"claims": [ {"claim": str, "candidate_ids": [str],
                  "span_start": int>=0, "span_end": int>=1} ]}   max 4 claims
  Each claim is tied to a character span of the source chunk and to entity
  candidates found in that chunk. Malformed output gets one repair attempt.
  Of 6,431 requests, 6,431 were accepted and 0 quarantined (GPU manifest).

  Entity candidates: 34,813 mentions (types include citations, named
  entities and controlled terms such as LGBTQ, SOGIESC, sexual orientation,
  gender identity, transgender, homophobia).

3.4 Graph schema
  Node types: Document, Page, EvidenceChunk, Claim, Entity
  (+ Mention in the retrieval index).
  Edge types: document_has_page, page_has_chunk, chunk_asserts_claim,
  claim_mentions_entity, claim_related_to_claim.
  Size:
    Viewer graph: 35,618 nodes (90 documents, 5,997 pages, 6,431 chunks,
    21,592 claims, 1,508 entities) and 67,664 edges (21,592
    chunk-asserts-claim, 17,576 claim-mentions-entity, 16,068
    claim-related-to-claim, 6,431 page-has-chunk, 5,997 document-has-page).
    1,103 claims (5.1%) have spans that do not match the source text.
    Retrieval-index graph (after validation and entity canonicalisation):
    1,416 entities, 25,697 mentions, 35,485 edges over 6,431 evidence chunks.

3.5 Integrity
  Every stage (corpus, chunks, extraction, ledger) has a stage hash, and
  downstream stages refuse to run if hashes do not match. The evidence
  ledger is bound to the semantic stage hash.


================================================================================
4. HYBRID RETRIEVAL
================================================================================
4.1 Models (pinned revisions)
  Dense encoder: BAAI/bge-m3 (rev 5617a9f6...), 1,024-dimensional,
                 L2-normalised, cosine similarity.
  Reranker:      BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3 (rev 953dc6f6...), cross-encoder,
                 max length 512.
  Lexical:       BM25 (Okapi), tokens = lower-cased [\w']+.
  Vector store:  Qdrant (local mode), exact cosine search.

4.2 Query signature (entity linking of the post)
  For each post, the LLM receives the target, the post and a closed list of
  catalogue candidates for the target. It returns
    {entity_candidate_indices: [{mention_id, candidate_index|null}],
     predicates[], polarities[], modalities[], desired_stances[]}
  It never writes entity IDs itself; the selected indices are resolved to
  catalogue entity IDs. If a post links to no entity, retrieval abstains
  ("no_linked_entities").

4.3 Three retrieval branches
  Dense: query "evidence addressing harmful or misleading claims about
         {target}", top 12, cosine >= 0.25.
  BM25:  query "{target} {post}", top 12, score > 0.
  Graph: seeds = dense("evidence addressing {target} {post}") + BM25 seeds,
         each branch min-max normalised; breadth-first traversal over typed
         edges up to 2 hops; top 8.

  Graph score of an evidence chunk e (weighted sum of 10 components):
    S(e) = 2.0*[entity match] + 1.0*[predicate match] + 0.5*[polarity match]
         + 0.4*[modality match] + 0.8*[stance match] + 0.8*review(e)
         + 0.8*authority(e) + 0.0*confidence(e) + 1.0*seed(e)
         + 0.6*(0.75^h)
    [x] = 1 if any claim connected to e matches the query signature, else 0
    review: accepted 1.0, reviewed 0.85, unknown 0.0
    authority in [0, 1]
    seed = best normalised dense/BM25 seed score
    h = shortest hop distance from a seed
    extraction confidence is recorded but weighted 0 (uncalibrated).
  Rules: a hop-0 seed without claims, or without an entity or predicate
  match, is never a graph hit; chunks need S(e) > 0. Each result keeps its
  full path trace (nodes and relations) for auditing.

4.4 Fusion and reranking
  Weighted reciprocal rank fusion:
    RRF(d) = sum over b in {dense, bm25, graph} of  w_b / (k + rank_b(d))
    k = 60, w_dense = 1.1, w_bm25 = 1.0, w_graph = 0.8
  Cross-encoder scores each (post, chunk) pair; logits become
  probabilities p = sigmoid(logit).
  Selection: keep chunks with p >= 0.55, sort by p, take the top 5.
  If none pass, the system abstains
  ("all_candidates_below_minimum_rerank_probability").

4.5 Graph ablation
  The same retrieval is run with the graph branch on and off for every
  frozen ID. Both runs share the candidate universe. The metrics are overlap,
  graph-only gain, authority rate and accepted-evidence rate, compared with
  a paired permutation test (10,000 rounds).


================================================================================
5. GENERATION: FOUR VARIANTS
================================================================================
5.1 Generator
  Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B (rev 3764fa35...), 4-bit NF4 (Unsloth / bitsandbytes,
  effective 4-bit verified), max sequence 4,096 tokens, seed 3407,
  temperature 0.6, max 768 new tokens per stage.
  Thinking mode (two passes): pass 1 generates up to 192 reasoning tokens
  with thinking on; pass 2 adds that reasoning as private context and
  generates the final JSON with thinking off, under a JSON-schema constraint
  (lm-format-enforcer). The reasoning is stored separately from the answer.

5.2 Evidence ledger and prompt fitting
  Selected passages become a closed ledger E1..En (n <= 5). Each entry has
  evidence ID, source ID, page locator, document UID, character span,
  displayed text and SHA-256 hashes; the span text must equal the source
  substring exactly. If the prompt exceeds the 4,096-token budget, whole
  lowest-ranked passages are dropped (text is never cut mid-passage). If even
  the evidence-free prompt does not fit, the row is quarantined.

5.3 Variants
  (a) Zero-shot: post + target -> final JSON. No evidence.
  (b) Few-shot: as (a) plus 2 fixed synthetic examples, written separately
      from the dataset so no test content can leak into the prompt
      ("few-shot.v1.contamination-safe.static").
  (c) KG-RAG: post + target + evidence ledger + a fixed plan
      {claim_focus: "Address the claim respectfully.", tone: empathetic,
      safety_constraints: [avoid amplification]} -> final JSON with citations.
  (d) MP-KG-RAG: 5 perspective agents -> planner -> final synthesiser.

5.4 MP-KG-RAG stages (7 generation calls per row)
  Perspective agents (5 calls), each given the post, target, category and
  ledger:
    fact_checking     "Correct only directly supported claims."
    cultural_context  "Give respectful context."
    harm_reduction    "Avoid amplifying harmful language."
    legal_rights      "Use only source-grounded rights claims."
    persuasion        "Suggest an empathetic response."
    Output: {perspective, rationale, claims_to_address[],
             supported_evidence_ids[E#], response_guidance[], risk_flags[],
             confidence in [0, 1]}
    Gate: at least 98% of the perspective outputs must parse.
  Planner (1 call): input = the parsed perspectives (falling back to a
    compact version if over budget) + ledger.
    Output: {claim_focus, selected_evidence_ids[E#], response_steps[], tone,
             factual_constraints[], safety_constraints[]}
  Final synthesiser (1 call): input = plan + ledger.
    Instruction: factual claims need inline [E#] citations; with no factual
    claims, return one approved safe-abstention template and set
    factual_claims to [].
    Output: {counter_narrative, cited_evidence_ids[E#], factual_claims[],
             safety_notes[]}
  Grounding repair (only if citation checks fail): one extra call with the
  failure reasons; re-validated.

5.5 Prompt templates (verbatim)
  Perspective, system: "Think carefully, then return exactly one JSON object
    after the thinking block."
  Perspective, user: "Perspective: {name}\nGoal: {goal}\nPost: {post}\n
    Target: {target}\nCategory: {category}\nEvidence (complete ledger spans
    only):\n{ledger}\nReturn perspective, rationale, claims_to_address,
    supported_evidence_ids, response_guidance, risk_flags, confidence. Use
    only E1...En IDs.\nJSON schema (must be followed exactly): {schema}"
  Planner, system: "Return one JSON evidence plan only."
  Final, system: "Write one safe evidence-grounded counter-narrative as JSON
    only."
  Baseline, system: "Return exactly one JSON object matching the final
    response schema. Prompt variant: {zero-shot.v1 | few-shot.v1...}."
  Ledger line format:
    "[E#] source={source_id} p. {page} document_uid={uid}
     span={start}:{end}\n{passage}"

5.6 Robustness rules
  - One JSON repair attempt (temperature 0, no thinking); after that the row
    is quarantined with a reason (schema_invalid, prompt_budget,
    parse_rate, citation_support_failed) and kept out of the results.
  - Missing text never becomes "nan"; missing scores never become 0 (status
    fields record why a value is missing).
  - Append-only per-variant JSONL checkpoints with identity hashes allow a
    run to resume safely.


================================================================================
6. CITATION VALIDATION
================================================================================
6.1 Format checks (all RAG outputs)
  - Cited IDs must exist in the closed ledger E1..En; no duplicates.
  - Inline [E#] markers must exactly match cited_evidence_ids.
  - A response that declares factual claims must contain inline citations.

6.2 Entailment checks
  NLI model: joeddav/xlm-roberta-large-xnli (rev 07f8772b...), labels
  contradiction / neutral / entailment.
  Each CN sentence is a claim. For a claim c with cited passages P:
    joint support    = entail(concatenation of P, c) >= threshold
    leave-one-out    = for each p in P, entail(P minus p, c); p is
                       "necessary" if support drops below the threshold
  Reported per response: citation precision, citation recall (share of
  factual claims supported), citation necessity, over-citation count
  (cited but unnecessary passages), entailment mean.
  Safe abstention: the system may refuse with an approved template ("I cannot
  verify this from the available evidence.", or its Hindi / Tamil
  equivalent); such a response must contain no citations.

6.3 Per-language threshold calibration
  Calibration data: English facebook/xnli (validation);
  Hindi and Tamil mteb/IndicXnliPairClassification (test).
  600 stratified examples per language, at least 50 per label, 2,000
  bootstrap resamples. The threshold maximises F1. The calibration is
  accepted only if the lower confidence bounds of accuracy, entailment
  precision and entailment recall are all >= 0.70.


================================================================================
7. EVALUATION PROTOCOL
================================================================================
7.1 Automatic metrics (CN vs reference CN; per language)
  chrF      Unicode character n-gram F-score, n = 6, beta = 2 (suits
            Devanagari and code-mixed text).
  BERTScore FacebookAI/xlm-roberta-large (rev 91703fe2...), multilingual.
  ROUGE-L   F-measure.
  METEOR    whitespace tokenisation.
  Toxicity  Detoxify, on CPU (English model; lower is better).
  Citation  precision, recall, necessity, entailment (RAG variants only;
            "not applicable" for the baselines).
  Language and script checks: output language vs input language; rows
  without a reference are excluded and counted.

7.2 Statistical testing
  Comparisons: MP-KG-RAG vs KG-RAG; MP-KG-RAG vs zero-shot;
  KG-RAG vs zero-shot.
  Test: paired sign-permutation test on the shared ID set (10,000
  permutations). Multiple comparisons: Benjamini-Hochberg FDR.
  Uncertainty: bootstrap 95% confidence intervals of the mean.
  Each metric records its direction (toxicity: lower is better).

7.3 Human evaluation
  Sample: up to 200 IDs, stratified by Category | Target | script, all
  variants. Responses are blinded (R0001...) with a separate key file.
  10 criteria (ordinal scale): politeness_respect, helpfulness,
  factuality, safety, empathy, fluency, inclusivity, non_confrontational,
  contextual_coherence, persuasiveness.
  At least 2 distinct raters. Agreement: quadratic weighted Cohen's kappa
  and ordinal Krippendorff's alpha.

9. OBSERVATIONS FROM THE LOCAL DEPLOYMENT (QUALITATIVE ONLY)
================================================================================
  Do not report these as benchmark results (different model, single queries).

  Case 1 (English): "I don't think same-sex couples should be allowed to
  adopt children."
    Retrieval: 5 passages kept (E1-E5). All 10 stages completed.
    Citation validation passed (no unknown IDs).
    The CN cited legal recognition of joint adoption by same-sex couples in
    28 UN member states and Yogyakarta Principle 24(a).
    Latency on CPU: 5 min 29 s end to end (about 70-90 s per perspective /
    plan / final call); final stage 3,628 prompt tokens, 346 generated
    tokens, about 9 tokens/s.

  Case 2 (Romanized Hinglish): "Gay log bimar hote hain, inka ilaaj hona
  chahiye. LGBTQ log samaj ke liye khatra hain."
    Retrieval abstained: no candidate reached p >= 0.55. The system refused
    rather than generate an ungrounded reply. This shows the safety
    behaviour, but also a coverage gap: Romanized Hinglish queries match
    the English-language evidence corpus poorly.

  Observed generation behaviour: the 4B model often listed evidence IDs in
  cited_evidence_ids without inline markers. The pipeline then appends
  "Sources: [E1] ... [E5]", which gives response-level rather than
  claim-level attribution.

  Cost: building the dense index for 6,431 chunks on CPU took about 1.3 s
  per chunk in a benchmark, but 11 hours in practice (thermal throttling).
  It is a one-time cost.

10. LIMITATIONS AND ETHICS
================================================================================
  - Cross-lingual gap: the evidence corpus is mostly English, while 64% of
    posts are Romanized Hinglish and 36% Devanagari. Retrieval abstains
    more often on Hinglish. Possible fixes: transliteration or translation
    of the query before retrieval, or Hindi sources in the corpus.
  - The dataset is imbalanced: 9.4% non-homophobic rows, with label noise
    ("None" vs "No Target"; 22 duplicate references).
  - Reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR) penalise valid CNs that
    differ from the single reference. Weight chrF, BERTScore, citation
    support and human ratings accordingly.
  - NLI-based support checks a claim against the cited text only; they do
    not establish real-world truth. Detoxify is English-only.
  - The LLM-extracted graph contains errors (5.1% of claims have invalid
    spans). Extraction confidence is uncalibrated and therefore unweighted.
  - Ethics: the dataset contains synthetic hateful language for research
    and moderation purposes. No real persons, handles or private data;
    no operational harm content. Release with a content warning and a
    research-use licence. The CN generator is meant to assist human
    moderators, not to reply automatically without review.
  - No model was trained or fine-tuned. All gains come from retrieval,
    grounding and multi-perspective prompting.
